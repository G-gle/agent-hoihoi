What these numbers tell us

These records represent requests that reached the web server.

They include repeated requests from the same visitor and automated crawling.

The numbers therefore cannot tell us how many people visited, or whether any of them were AI agents.

How the counts are compiled

Each GET reaching the Worker for Home (/ or /index.html), Research (/research.html), Field observations (/observations.html), Guestbook (/guestbook.html), Article 1 (/agent-traps.html), or Article 2 (/agents-and-traps.html) counts once when its write succeeds. All response statuses from 100 through 599 are included, including redirects and errors. HEAD and other methods, About, this detail page, the receipt page, assets and APIs are excluded. Dates change at 00:00 UTC. Query strings do not create separate page categories. Administrator and test requests are not automatically excluded.

A daily job deletes raw observation records older than 90 days. Daily page aggregates have no automatic expiry. These pages read only daily aggregates. Individual request records, User-Agent values, referring domains and pending guestbook messages are not displayed.

Reloads, bots and prefetches can all contribute to counts. Counts alone cannot establish arrival from Research, link clicks or visitor intent.

Recording happens asynchronously after the page response is prepared. The current overview request is not included in its displayed snapshot and may appear on a later retrieval. Access-blocked requests, browser-only cache views, recording pauses and failed writes can be absent. Retained aggregates do not establish continuous observation or a complete history.
